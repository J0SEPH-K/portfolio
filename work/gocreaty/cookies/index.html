Overview

This Cookie Policy explains how GoCreaty ("we", "us", "our") uses cookies and similar browser-storage technologies on our website and web application (the "Service"). It should be read together with our Privacy Policy, which describes how we handle personal information more generally. GoCreaty is operated from the United States and offers the Service to users in the United States.

GoCreaty uses a deliberately minimal set of first-party cookies and browser storage. We do not use third-party advertising, retargeting, or cross-site tracking cookies, we do not load Google Analytics or any third-party analytics or advertising pixel, and we do not sell or share your personal information, including for cross-context behavioral advertising as those terms are defined under the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA). The only third-party cookies that may be set in connection with the Service are those placed by Stripe on Stripe's own pages when you make a payment, as described below.

In short: GoCreaty relies on a small number of strictly-necessary first-party cookies so you can sign in and stay signed in, a small amount of browser local storage to hold your access token and preferences, and Stripe's own checkout cookies during payment. There are no advertising or cross-site tracking cookies, and we do not sell or share your personal information.

What cookies and local storage are

A cookie is a small text file that a website asks your browser to store on your device. On each later request, the browser sends the cookie back, which lets the site recognize your session. Cookies can be first-party (set by the site you are visiting — here, GoCreaty) or third-party (set by another domain, such as a payment provider).

Local storage and session storage are related browser technologies that let a site store small pieces of data in your browser. Unlike cookies, this data is read by code running in the page and is not automatically attached to every network request. GoCreaty uses a small amount of local storage and session storage to keep your in-page session and preferences, as listed below.

Throughout this policy we refer to all of these — cookies, local storage, and session storage — as "cookies and similar technologies." For each technology we tell you its name or category, whether it is set by GoCreaty (first-party) or by a third party, what it does, and how long it lasts. The categories of personal information we collect through these technologies are limited to identifiers (for example, session tokens and a theme/preference value) and internet or other electronic network activity (for example, the pages you view on the Service), used solely to provide, secure, and improve the Service.

1. Strictly-necessary cookies and storage (no consent required)

These technologies are essential to provide the Service you ask for and cannot be turned off in our systems. They are set only in response to actions you take, such as signing in. Without them, secure sign-in and staying signed in cannot be provided. Because they are strictly necessary to deliver a service you explicitly request, they do not require your consent — but we describe them here for transparency.

Authentication and session cookies (first-party)

  • gc_refresh — First-party, HttpOnly refresh-token cookie. It carries your app refresh token so the application can quietly obtain short-lived access tokens on your behalf, keeping you signed in without repeated logins. Because it is HttpOnly, page scripts cannot read it, which protects your session. It is set when you sign in, rotated each time it is used, and deleted when you log out, when a refresh fails, or when your session is revoked. It is sent only over HTTPS (Secure) in production. Category: strictly-necessary authentication. Duration: up to 30 days (renewed as you keep using the Service).
  • gc_oauth_state — First-party, HttpOnly, short-lived cookie set only during Google sign-in. It protects the login round-trip against cross-site request forgery by binding the sign-in request to your browser, and is cleared as soon as sign-in completes. Without it, sign-in cannot complete. Category: strictly-necessary security. Duration: about 10 minutes.

Browser local storage (first-party)

  • gocreaty-token — A first-party local storage entry that holds your short-lived access token (a JSON Web Token) so that an open browser tab keeps your signed-in session in memory across page reloads until the next refresh. It is cleared when you log out. Category: strictly-necessary authentication. Duration: until you log out or clear your browser storage.
  • gc_theme — A first-party local storage entry that remembers your light/dark theme preference so the interface does not flash the wrong theme on load. It contains no personal information beyond your stated preference. Category: strictly-necessary functionality. Duration: until you change it or clear your browser storage.
  • gocreaty-active-channel — A first-party local storage entry that remembers which view or filter you last selected in the trending feed, so the feed reopens the way you left it. It contains no personal information and is written only if you make such a selection. Category: strictly-necessary functionality. Duration: until you change it or clear your browser storage.

Session storage used during sign-in (first-party, one-time)

  • gc_consent — A first-party session storage entry that briefly carries the consent choices you make at sign-up (acceptance of our Terms and Privacy Policy) so they can be recorded against your account. It is used once and not persisted across browser sessions. Category: strictly-necessary functionality.
  • gc_ref — A first-party session storage entry that briefly carries a referral invite code through the sign-in flow so a referral can be applied once after you create your account. It is used once and not persisted across browser sessions. Category: strictly-necessary functionality.

Because these cookies and storage entries are strictly necessary to deliver a service you explicitly request — secure sign-in, staying signed in, and recording your consent — they require no consent under applicable U.S. state privacy law and we do not display a consent banner for them. We disclose them here so you have a complete and accurate picture of what GoCreaty stores in your browser and why.

2. First-party analytics signal (planned, not active; no advertising, no profiling)

Status: planned and not currently active. The live application does not emit page-view beacons and loads no analytics tracker. This section describes a first-party capability we may enable in the future so you know in advance exactly how it would work.

GoCreaty's design includes the ability to count aggregate page views using a first-party signal, so we can understand, in aggregate, how many people visit the Service and which pages are used, in order to improve the product. This is a first-party capability only: any such data would be processed by us internally, would not be shared with any advertising network or third party, would not be used to build a profile of you or to track you across other websites, and would not be used for advertising. We do not use Google Analytics or any third-party analytics or advertising pixel.

If and where this first-party page-view signal is activated, it would record only a randomly-generated client identifier used to count distinct visitors (explicitly not a device fingerprint), the page path you visited (with query strings removed), whether the event is a view or a presence heartbeat, a timestamp, and — for signed-in users — your account identifier. It would be stored only on our own systems, used solely to power an internal usage dashboard, and retained for no longer than 24 months, after which it would be deleted or aggregated into non-identifying counts. If we activate this signal we will update this policy to name any cookie or storage key it sets and confirm its retention period.

Because we do not use this signal for advertising, profiling, or any sale or sharing of personal information, activating it would not require a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link or a consent banner under U.S. state privacy law. We disclose it in advance as a matter of transparency and FTC-consistent honesty about what the Service can do.

3. Payment cookies set by Stripe (third-party, on Stripe's domain)

When you subscribe or manage billing, payment and checkout are handled by Stripe, Inc., our payment processor, on Stripe's own pages and domain (for example, checkout.stripe.com and Stripe's billing portal). In that process, Stripe sets and reads its own cookies — including strictly-necessary cookies for fraud prevention, security, and completing your transaction — under Stripe's control and its own cookie policy, not GoCreaty's. We do not control and cannot read those cookies.

Stripe acts as our service provider and processes your payment information under a written agreement that restricts Stripe from selling or sharing that information or using it for its own purposes. Under the CCPA, this service-provider arrangement is not a "sale" or "share" of your personal information. Stripe's own collection of information through its pages and cookies is governed by Stripe's own policies.

  • These cookies are set by Stripe, a third party, on Stripe's domain; GoCreaty does not set, read, or control them.
  • Stripe is responsible for any consent required for any non-essential cookies it sets on its own pages.
  • GoCreaty does not receive or store your full payment card number. We store only Stripe identifiers (such as customer and subscription IDs); your card details remain with Stripe, which is PCI-compliant.

For details of the cookies Stripe sets and the choices available to you, see Stripe's Cookie Policy at Stripe Cookies Policy and Stripe's Privacy Policy.

4. No third-party advertising or cross-site tracking cookies

GoCreaty does not use any third-party advertising, retargeting, or cross-site tracking cookies or pixels. We do not load Google Analytics, Google Ads, the Meta Pixel, the TikTok pixel, or any similar third-party advertising or analytics technology in the Service. The only third-party cookies in the picture are Stripe's, set on Stripe's own domain during checkout as described above.

Because we do not share your personal information with third parties for cross-context behavioral advertising, and we do not sell your personal information, under the CCPA we are not required to provide a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link, and there is no advertising opt-out for you to make on GoCreaty. If our practices ever change so that we sell or share personal information, we will update this policy, post any opt-out mechanism that the law then requires, and automatically detect and honor browser opt-out preference signals at that time.

5. Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track

GoCreaty recognizes the Global Privacy Control (GPC) and other browser-based opt-out preference signals, and our Privacy Policy describes how we honor them. Because we do not sell or share your personal information and do not use cookies for cross-context behavioral advertising, there is no advertising or tracking activity for a GPC or "Do Not Track" signal to stop — so no opt-out action is required, and your information is already not sold or shared whether or not you enable GPC.

If our practices ever change so that we do sell or share personal information, we will automatically detect and honor GPC signals as a valid request to opt out, without requiring you to verify your identity, and we will provide a way for you to confirm your opt-out was processed.

6. How to control cookies — and what happens if you block them

You can control and delete cookies and browser storage through your browser settings. Most browsers let you view stored cookies and local storage, delete them individually or in bulk, and block some or all cookies. Where to find these controls varies by browser — see the help pages for Chrome, Safari, Firefox, or Edge.

Important: GoCreaty's authentication cookies are strictly necessary. If you block or delete the gc_refresh cookie or clear the gocreaty-token local storage entry, you will be signed out and will not be able to sign in or stay signed in until you allow them again. Blocking the gc_oauth_state cookie will prevent Google sign-in from completing. Blocking these cookies does not give you a more private experience on GoCreaty, because they are not used for advertising or tracking — only to operate your session.

Clearing the gc_theme or gocreaty-active-channel entries simply resets those preferences. Cookies set by Stripe on Stripe's pages are managed through your browser and through the choices Stripe offers on its own domain.
