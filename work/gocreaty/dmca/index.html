DMCA & Copyright Policy

Last updated: June 26, 2026

Our Respect for Intellectual Property

GoCreaty ('we', 'us', 'our') respects the intellectual property rights of others and expects the people who use our service to do the same. This policy explains how we respond to notices of alleged copyright infringement under the U.S. Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA), 17 U.S.C. § 512, and how copyright owners and our users can submit copyright notices and counter-notifications. GoCreaty is operated as a United States business and serves users in the United States. This policy is part of, and incorporated into, our Terms of Service.

This policy applies to: (1) videos you upload to GoCreaty for your own analysis (a paid feature available on the Pro and Max plans); and (2) the public trending short-form video data we surface from third-party platforms. We address each of these below, because the legal treatment of user uploads differs from the treatment of public third-party content.

How GoCreaty handles third-party public videos

For trending YouTube Shorts and TikTok videos created by other people, GoCreaty surfaces publicly available metadata (such as titles, view, like and comment counts, creator names and handles, and thumbnail images) and links back to the original video on the source platform. We generate AI analysis of why a video may be trending. We do not re-host or re-serve those creators' video files: YouTube videos are analyzed by reference to their public watch URL, and TikTok video files, when fetched at all, are retrieved transiently and are never stored or served by GoCreaty. Creator avatars and thumbnails are displayed by hotlinking to the source, not by copying them onto our own servers. Third-party trending data is automatically purged from our systems after approximately 30 days.

Because the trending data we surface is link-and-metadata only rather than re-hosted video, our handling of third-party content is closer to the information-location-tool framing of 17 U.S.C. § 512(d) than to the hosting framing of § 512(c). We do not store or serve creators' underlying video files; we reference them on the platform that hosts them.

How GoCreaty handles your own uploads

If you are a Pro or Max subscriber, you may upload your own short-form video to be analyzed by our AI. You are responsible for ensuring you hold the necessary rights to any video you upload. Uploaded files are stored on GoCreaty's own backend infrastructure (our server local disk) so you can revisit your analysis history, and they are deleted when you delete the upload or your account. The DMCA notice-and-takedown and counter-notification procedures below apply to this user-uploaded content (User Content), which is hosted by GoCreaty and therefore falls within the § 512(c) hosting framework.

Reporting Copyright Infringement (DMCA Takedown Notice)

If you are a copyright owner, or are authorized to act on behalf of one, and you believe that material available through GoCreaty infringes your copyright, you may submit a written DMCA takedown notice to our Designated Copyright Agent at the contact details below. To be effective under 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3), your notice must include substantially all of the following:

  • (1) Signature. Your physical or electronic signature, as the owner of an exclusive right that is allegedly infringed, or as a person authorized to act on the owner's behalf.
  • (2) Identification of the work. Identification of the copyrighted work you claim has been infringed — or, if your notice covers multiple works at a single online location, a representative list of those works.
  • (3) Identification and location of the material. Identification of the material you claim is infringing and that you want removed or disabled, together with information reasonably sufficient to let us locate it — for example, the URL or other specific location of the material on GoCreaty.
  • (4) Your contact information. Your name, mailing address, telephone number, and, if available, an email address so we can contact you.
  • (5) Good-faith statement. A statement that you have a good-faith belief that the use of the material in the manner complained of is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
  • (6) Statement of accuracy and authority, under penalty of perjury. A statement that the information in your notice is accurate, and, under penalty of perjury, that you are the owner of, or authorized to act on behalf of the owner of, the exclusive right that is allegedly infringed.

Before sending a notice, you must consider whether the material is a fair use. Under Lenz v. Universal Music Corp. (9th Cir. 2015), a copyright owner must form a good-faith belief that the use is unauthorized — including considering fair use — before requesting removal.

Please send only properly directed copyright notices to our Designated Agent. This contact is for copyright notices only; notices that do not substantially comply with all six elements of 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3) may not be effective and may be ignored and may not receive a response. Other inquiries sent to this contact will not receive a response — for other concerns (for example, privacy, defamation, or general support), please contact us at legal@gocreaty.com instead.

Be careful what you swear to. Under 17 U.S.C. § 512(f), any person who knowingly materially misrepresents that material is infringing — or that material was removed or disabled by mistake or misidentification — may be liable for damages, including costs and attorneys' fees. We may reject bad-faith, abusive, or automated takedown spam that does not reflect a genuine, individualized review of the material. If you are unsure whether material infringes your rights, you may wish to consult an attorney before submitting a notice.

Transparency and forwarding. When we act on a notice or counter-notice, we may forward the complete notice or counter-notice — including your contact information and the substance of your claim — to the affected user, the opposing party, and/or third-party transparency databases (such as the Lumen database). Submitting a notice means you understand and consent to this forwarding.

Where a notice concerns third-party public content rather than a user upload, the most effective remedy is often to contact the source platform (for example, YouTube or TikTok) directly, since GoCreaty links to and surfaces metadata about content hosted on those platforms rather than hosting the underlying video files. You may also use the creator removal (opt-out) path above. We will nonetheless act on valid notices directed to material accessible through our service, including by removing infringing metadata or links where appropriate.

What Happens After We Receive a Notice

Upon receipt of a notice that substantially complies with 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3), we will act expeditiously to remove or disable access to the material claimed to be infringing. Where the material is a user upload, we will take reasonable steps to promptly notify the user who uploaded it that the material has been removed or disabled, and we will provide that user the opportunity to submit a counter-notification as described below.

No general duty to monitor; removal upon knowledge

GoCreaty does not actively monitor user-submitted material for infringement and has no obligation to do so. However, GoCreaty will act expeditiously to remove or disable access to material when it obtains actual knowledge that the material is infringing, becomes aware of facts or circumstances from which infringing activity is apparent (so-called 'red flag' awareness), or receives a notice substantially complying with 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3). GoCreaty does not receive a financial benefit directly attributable to infringing activity that it has the right and ability to control.

Our Designated Copyright Agent's published email (dmca@gocreaty.com) is monitored, and the notices and counter-notices we receive are date-stamped and logged so that we can act expeditiously and reasonably implement the repeat-infringer policy described below.

Counter-Notification (Restoring Removed Content)

If your uploaded content was removed or disabled and you believe this was the result of a mistake or misidentification, you may send a written counter-notification to our Designated Copyright Agent. To be effective under 17 U.S.C. § 512(g)(3), your counter-notification must include substantially all of the following:

  • (1) Signature. Your physical or electronic signature.
  • (2) Identification of the removed material. Identification of the material that was removed or disabled and the location at which it appeared before it was removed or disabled.
  • (3) Statement under penalty of perjury. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you have a good-faith belief that the material was removed or disabled as a result of mistake or misidentification.
  • (4) Your contact information and consent to jurisdiction. Your name, address, and telephone number, and a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the Federal District Court for the judicial district in which your address is located (or, if your address is outside the United States, any judicial district in which GoCreaty may be found), and that you will accept service of process from the person who filed the original DMCA notice, or that person's agent.

Put-back process and timing

Upon receiving a valid counter-notification, we will promptly forward it to the person who filed the original notice — including the contact information you provide — and inform them that we will restore the removed material in 10 business days. We will then restore the material not less than 10 nor more than 14 business days after we receive your counter-notification — unless our Designated Agent first receives notice from the original complainant that it has filed a court action seeking to restrain you from engaging in the allegedly infringing activity. The same penalty-of-perjury and misrepresentation cautions described above (17 U.S.C. § 512(f)) apply equally to counter-notifications: knowingly making a material misrepresentation in a counter-notification may subject you to liability.

Repeat-Infringer Policy

In accordance with 17 U.S.C. § 512(i), GoCreaty has adopted, and will reasonably implement, a policy providing for the termination, in appropriate circumstances, of the accounts of subscribers and account holders who are repeat infringers. We maintain an internal record (a strike log) of the infringement notices we receive against an account, and we may disable or terminate an account that accumulates repeated, unresolved valid notices, with or without prior warning. This policy is actually enforced, not merely stated.

Separately, and in addition to our repeat-infringer policy, GoCreaty may, in its sole discretion, limit access to or terminate the account of any user who infringes the intellectual property rights of others, whether or not there has been any repeat infringement.
